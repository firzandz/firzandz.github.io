Project 01

Grab and OVO Integration

Most of Superbank's users come through Grab and OVO — so the quality of the onboarding experience across those ecosystems directly determines whether acquisition converts into active users.

On the Grab side, I redesigned the account linkage flow, identifying the friction points driving drop-off and resolving them through clearer intent-setting and a tighter step sequence. The insight was simple but easy to miss: users weren't confused about Superbank — they were confused about why the handoff was happening and what they were agreeing to.

From there, I led the OVO Nabung experience end-to-end. The original registration required 21 fields. By reusing OVO's existing KYC data, we reduced it to 3 — with progressive compliance triggered only when users unlocked loan products. Less friction at entry, full compliance where it actually mattered. It shipped under a tight deadline, and became Superbank's onboarding standard for all future ecosystem partners.

Project 02

Enabling Payment Capabilities

Payment isn't a supporting feature in digital banking. It's the reason users come back — and the reason a bank generates revenue beyond deposits.

I built Superbank's payment layer from the ground up across four products: BI-Fast, QRIS, Bill Payment, and Cards. Each was designed not just to meet regulatory requirements, but to become a habitual touchpoint in users' daily financial lives. Without payment, Superbank is a place to park savings. With it, there's transaction fee revenue, there's engagement, and there's a sustainable business underneath.

But zero-to-one was only the foundation. Once the core products were live, I worked on extending them — integrating payment into the rewards ecosystem to drive retention, establishing it as a source of fund for open lending, and building a payment standardisation layer that every subsequent product could build on top of. The goal was to make payment the connective tissue of the entire Superbank experience, not just a standalone feature set.

Project 03

Lending Ecosystem Integration

Lending is Superbank's primary revenue driver — and most users arrive through Grab and OVO, not organic discovery. The challenge wasn't generating intent. It was converting it.

Channel 1: Joint Application Flow

I designed a joint application flow for Grab and OVO users, letting them register for both a loan and a Superbank account in one go, without leaving their ecosystem app. The design challenge was intent clarity: users needed to understand upfront that while they apply within the ecosystem, their loan would live in the Superbank app. Getting that handoff right was the difference between a confident user and a confused one.

Channel 2: Akulaku Loan Channeling

The second was a loan channeling integration with Akulaku, where users access credit backed by Superbank's limit through Akulaku's own platform. Here the challenge was compliance — Superbank's form requirements had to be completed mid-flow, on top of what Akulaku had already collected. The work was about making that feel like a natural continuation rather than a bureaucratic interruption, so users stayed motivated to complete and convert.

Both projects came down to the same underlying problem: high-intent users hitting low-clarity moments. The design work was about closing that gap.

Mentoring

Good design culture doesn't build itself.

Alongside my product work, I took on building design practice at Superbank from the inside. I ran sharing sessions on design process and craft, participated in internal hackathons, and mentored three designers — one of whom converted to a full-time role. Not as a formal mandate, but as something I believed the team needed.

The goal was simple: make design thinking more legible across the organisation, so it wasn't just designers who understood the value of the work — it was everyone we collaborated with.
