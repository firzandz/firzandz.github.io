The full story

The evidence, decisions, and tradeoffs behind the executive summary.

01. Frame the real problem

Give the minimum context required to understand the challenge. Avoid a long company introduction or a chronological diary of activities.

Use evidence. Include the behavior, data, or operational constraint that proved the visible interface problem was only a symptom.

02. Choose a direction

Show the model, principle, or insight that gave the team a shared direction. This section should make your judgment visible.

  • Decision 01Name the move and explain the tradeoff it resolved.
  • Decision 02Connect the design choice to user and business evidence.
  • Decision 03Explain how the solution could scale beyond the first release.

03. Make the change tangible

Use a small number of annotated product artifacts. Each visual should prove a decision rather than simply decorate the page.

04. Close the loop

Return to the outcomes from the trailer and explain what changed for users, the business, and the team.

  1. XX% — primary product or user result.
  2. X× — business, operational, or efficiency result.
  3. Y months — adoption, rollout, or delivery evidence.

My reflection

End with the lesson you still use. Name what you would keep, what you would change, and how the project shaped your practice.

Avoid generic lessons. The strongest reflection reveals a specific change in how you make product decisions.

Template writing order
  1. Write the outcomes first.
  2. Summarize the situation and your contribution.
  3. Select two or three decisions that prove the summary.
  4. Add only the artifacts required to explain those decisions.
  5. Finish with one honest reflection.
